# CDAS course proposal: substantive responses

**Proposed course title:** R for Social Sciences: Data Management and Programming for Quantitative Research

**Status:** Draft for review. This document covers the three substantive writing sections of the CDAS Course Proposal Form. Administrative fields—including course number, enrollment, units, format, and grading basis—are separate.

## 1. Course Description

*Form prompt: “Course Description (to be published to the General Catalog, please keep to a maximum of 1080 characters)”*

Data management and programming methods for quantitative research using R. Understanding data as objects and developing programming foundations to investigate, manipulate, and analyze them. Integrating conceptual reasoning and programming to produce transparent, reproducible, analysis-ready datasets. No prior programming experience required.

## 2. Justification

*Form prompt: “Justification (brief description of why this request is being made)”*

We request permission to renumber the already-approved EDUC M45 as an upper-division EDUC course so that it can fulfill the undergraduate major’s Inquiry and Design requirements. The course content will remain unchanged.

The course teaches essential research design skills by helping students develop systematic approaches to investigating, manipulating, and analyzing data. To date, quantitative courses within Inquiry and Design have attracted less interest among students in the major. One possible explanation is students’ discomfort with R as a programming language. This course addresses that barrier through a tested, rigorous, and supportive introduction to R. Its graduate-level version, designed for students new to both programming and statistics, has been popular and has helped students develop the skills and confidence to pursue subsequent statistics courses. Including this course within Inquiry and Design would provide undergraduate students with an accessible foundation for quantitative research and further methodological training.

The course will be M-listed with other departments, and we anticipate that it will also be popular with students minoring in Education and students majoring in related social science fields.

## 3. Alignment of Course with Ed&IS Guiding Principles

This course aligns with Ed&IS’s commitment to inquiry, knowledge advancement, and the improvement of professional practice by equipping students with essential data literacy and computational skills relevant to education and information professions. It prepares students to critically assess the role of data in shaping educational and societal outcomes and to make informed decisions in their fields. Additionally, this course is designed to welcome students who have traditionally been excluded from data and programming instruction. Through lectures (live and recorded), interactive materials that require students to be active learners, opportunities for collaborative problem solving, and structured peer support, the course advances the department’s commitment to diversity, social justice, and an ethic of caring while expanding access to these essential skills for students across backgrounds.
